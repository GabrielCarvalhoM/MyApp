# WEEK_COMPOSITION_CONTRACT — R7-A.1

**Revision:** R7A1-COMPOSITION-2, 2026-10-27 (the text frozen with R7A1-FREEZE-A2; it extends R7A1-COMPOSITION-1 of FREEZE-A1 with the correction cycle of its reviews, marked *A2*). **Status:** engineering contract of the offline synthetic Coaching Core (SHADOW reference). It states how the existing semantics compose; it adds no sporting rule. Sporting content is cited from the current policy (`R6-POLICY-5`, hash `18ec48f3…`) and the SSCTR records, and the items that change a dose are sent to the SSCTR.

## Why a contract

On R7A-FREEZE-2 every handler re-planned the week from its own subset of the state. The weekly review started next week from the evidence or the return; the re-plan from next week started from the evidence or HOLD; the equipment change and the re-plan from this week started from the projection of the week start; the return started from the pause week. Same-week adaptations lived only in rows, and context changes overwrote the global context. The seven R7-A blockers and ten more paths of the same class (task contract) follow from that. The fix is one composition function, `composeWeeks` in `CoachingCore/src/r6-composition.mjs`, that every re-planning handler calls, plus one way to apply a same-week adaptation and one way to apply a context change.

## The contract

**C1 — History (I1).** A session that is not *ahead* of the review keeps its row exactly. *Ahead* has one definition for every handler (`aheadOf`): dated after the review's local date, or dated that day with no execution report yet while its shift is not over. The plan knows only the athlete's chosen shift: a morning (AM) session is over at noon local time; an afternoon or unspecified shift stays ahead until the day ends (QE-R7A-02 same-day eligibility, QE-R7A-23). Every adaptation selector (fatigue, unavailable day, event conflict, the slots a move may use) uses this one test alone; re-applied later, an adaptation reaches only what was still ahead at its own report (it records the sessions of its day already past) (*A2*, QE-R7A1-04). A re-plan never removes or rewrites a past row (final validation `pastSessionRewritten/Removed`). Rows of one slot keep the order of the new plan, whichever sports the change re-planned.

**C2 — Context scope (I2, INV-11).** Schedule, goals and event, equipment, templates, phases, blocks and arrangements are the *context*. A change applies from the week it is stated for: this week's remaining days (equipment, an event change that reaches this week), next week (schedule, goal, learned arrangement), a later week (a dated schedule or goal change: the first week that begins on or after its date), or a date window (equipment windows; a permanent change from a later date is an open window from that date). The program keeps the earlier context in force for the weeks before a change (`contextBefore`), and every week is built and validated under the context in force for it (`skeleton.at(week)`). The transition into a week follows that week's context: a change from a later week starts that week from the week before it as planned (assumed executed), under the new context of that week. A newer statement about the same window wins (equipment windows: the latest matching window). The equipment in force on a date is one reading for the templates, the moves and the final validation, and an equipment statement is recorded even when nothing needs a substitute at the time, so a later plan or move keeps it (*A2*, QE-R7A1-01).

**C3 — Starts (I2, I7).** The start of the current week is its stored start state; a return that re-plans this week replaces it. The start of next week follows **one rule for every handler** (weekly review, re-plan from next week, re-plan of this week, return):
- a return that re-plans from next week is next week's start (its re-base), and every later change of this week keeps it;
- otherwise it is this week's transition into next week's context: **gated by the week's evidence once the week is complete**, read with the weekly review's own inputs by every handler (the systemic determination, a recorded `SYSTEMIC_SIGNAL` or converging reports, and every load confirmation recorded on the week, `LOAD_CONFIRMATIONS`) (*A2*, QE-R7A1-02), (no session pending; the weekly review's own rule), and **while the week is under way, the conditional plan of the week as now planned** — its kept and new rows before same-week adaptations, assumed executed as prescribed, as every later week is planned — except the sessions dated before a return reported this week, which belong to the plan before the break (C5).
A change from a later week (a dated change) leaves the weeks before it as planned, and as earlier weeks of the conditional plan their runs count as executed for the single-run ceiling, exactly as a re-plan from next week counts them (policy `run.singleRunCeiling`: "in a conditional projection, earlier projected weeks count as executed").
On R7A-FREEZE-2 the re-plan from next week held every step mid-week while the re-plan of this week used the conditional plan of rows the new context would have planned for days already past; a later change of this week could then rewrite a start a decision had set.

**C4 — Adaptations (I7).** A same-week change that only alters rows is an adaptation: systemic or local fatigue, an unavailable day, a missed-session move, an event-week conflict, the wait of a return that re-plans from next week, and the easy first quality slot of a short return. It is applied to the week as it stands and recorded on that week (`week.adaptations`) as a fact with its own clock. Whenever the week is re-planned, its recorded adaptations are applied again, in order, each with its own clock, to the re-planned rows only; history rows and the rows of sports outside the change already carry them. An adaptation never changes another week or a state (I2). A move whose slot is already history is consumed and is not applied again. A move never uses a slot whose session was skipped (an unavailable or event-conflict date), nor a date where its content needs equipment the athlete has not got then; a recorded move that can no longer be applied is dropped and the session stays missed, without debt (*A2*); and content an adaptation moves into a slot keeps every earlier adaptation of the week (a fatigue report, a short return's easy slot, a wait), applied again to that slot with its own clock. A systemic fatigue report with nothing left to reduce reviews the week with its signal and records it (`SYSTEMIC_SIGNAL`), so a later review of the same week keeps it. A waiting session is rest by design (no skip status), and an event-period conflict skips sessions only, never a day that has none. Two adaptations of the same week each apply to the week as it stands when reported, so their order can decide which slot carries a move or a skip (with a short return, which quality slot runs easy, C5); every restriction of both holds and every session stays at or below its planned dose either way (checked by both handler grids in every such pair). A week's planned totals describe its rows as planned, before same-week adaptations (as before R7-A.1).

**C5 — Supersession (I3).** A newer fact of the same kind replaces the older: a return report replaces the adaptations of an earlier return of the same weeks; a newer statement of the event date is the event, and an event change that reaches this week re-plans this week's remaining days, so the earlier race session does not stay beside the new one (final validation: at most one race ahead); the latest equipment window that covers a date is in force on it, and a permanent equipment change ends every earlier window on the day before it starts. A newer report of a break that began this week starts from the start this week had before an earlier return of it replaced that start (the week keeps it, `startBeforeReturn`). An event change re-plans this week's remaining days when it moves one of them into **or out of** the taper, race or post-event window: days count to the race (SSCTR S4), so the taper of a superseded date never stays beside the new one (sent to the SSCTR). A return supersedes the plan before the break: a session of that plan (dated before the return report, or dated that day and already past at it, C1 at the report's clock, *A2*) is never moved into the re-entry, and it never counts as done in the conditional plan of next week (R6-NONCLINICAL-RETURN; approved by the SSCTR, S5). A newer report of a break starts from the start the break's own week had before an earlier report replaced it, whichever week the newer report comes in (*A2*, QE-R7A1-03). After a short break the easy slot is the first quality slot still ahead of the report in the week as it stands (not one already skipped, nor one another adaptation of the week already eased), in the week re-entry starts or a later one, recorded on the week that holds it. Once made easy it stays the slot; one that a later change of its week skips or replaces passes on at the next composition from a later week (the weekly review at the latest). Exactly one slot changes (*A2*, SSCTR D58).

**C6 — Scope of the change (I2).** A change re-plans only the sports it concerns: an equipment change re-plans strength rows and the strength state; runs and the run state keep their values. Goal, event, schedule and return changes concern both sports.

**C7 — Final validation.** Every proposal passes the existing complete validation (prescription contract, chosen days and times of each week's own context, single key run per date, phase dose of every strength row of its day, race week present, establishment authority) plus at most one race ahead, equipment in force on each row's date (*A2*) and the immutability of history, or it is UNSUPPORTED (fail closed).

## Rules that are not composition (each cites its authority)

- **D57 (I6; SSCTR D57 required change, *A2*).** At and after the end of the return steps, the return cap rises one set per executed whole week (the D56 predicate) and ends only after such a week, once it covers the count the exercise would otherwise have next week: its normal count with the block-boundary set earned at that transition. So no count after a return comes back more than one set above the largest executed since. The regain cap already ends only when it covers every count.
- **D56 (I6; SSCTR D56 required change; formulation approved by the SSCTR: the last executed exposure).** The regain cap after a re-establishment and the return cap at and after the end of the return steps rise or end only after a week whose last executed exposure — the one that gives the week's counts (SSCTR R19, INV-04) — did its whole count (its count before the phase reduction) after its time fit. A time fit stays a non-reduction for the phase reading and for the return ceiling (R20). One reading for every representation: a row written before R7-A, which states no counts, reads its week's planned counts. Authority: `strength.setsAfterReestablishment`, `strengthReturnSets`, SSCTR REGAIN-BASE conditions 5–6, PLANNED-PROGRESSION 14, ROTATION 10, LOAD-ESTABLISHMENT 15. Sent to the SSCTR for the final formulation (whether "an executed exposure" means the last one, as here, or any one).
- **QE-R7A-12 (I5).** A logged load of an exposure that prescribed a known load is progression evidence: when it is lighter and adopted, the gate decides the repetition target (HOLD and REGRESS repeat it; ADVANCE takes the logged repetitions, as at the planned load). Only an establishment exposure (first establishment, re-establishment, return entry, from-log) sets its target from its logged repetitions. Authority: the gates (HOLD repeats the dose, REGRESS lowers the load) as the SSCTR read them in S2, and its R25 ("keep or clamp the target"). Sent to the SSCTR.
- **QE-R7A-02.** The short-return rule "the first quality slot after a short break runs easy" reads *ahead* as C1 defines it (SSCTR R27), and it is an adaptation (C4), so a later re-plan keeps it. Sent to the SSCTR.
- **QE-R7A-05 (I4).** Two agreeing reports of one session merge field by field: the entry chosen per exercise (more known values, then more sets, then the better declared source, then the more conservative values, then a fixed order) takes each load, repetition count or reserve it leaves unknown from the other report's same set. A value is known only when it can be read: a load in a known unit, a finite count; a present but unreadable value is filled the same way and kept as provenance (*A2*, QE-R7A1-05). No source wins by its label alone. Engineering only (no sporting rule).

## Answers to the Founder's ten questions

1. **Immutable history:** rows not ahead of the review (C1) and the append-only completed-work record (INV-10).
2. **Superseded:** an earlier return's adaptations and the week start it replaced, by a newer return of the same break; an earlier race and its event window, by the moved race; an earlier equipment window, by a newer window or a permanent change on the same dates; a corrected report, by its correction (INV-10) (C5).
3. **Local change:** an adaptation (C4) or a change whose scope is its sport and its weeks (C2, C6).
4. **Future commitment:** next week's start under the one rule (a return's re-base is kept; otherwise the evidence or the conditional plan of this week) (C3), and the context in force for each later week (C2).
5. **Survives a rebuild:** history, a return's re-base of next week, the adaptations, the context of each week, and the rows and states of sports outside the change.
6. **Two representations:** agreeing reports merge field by field, a known value never loses to an unknown one (QE-R7A-05); disagreeing reports are a conflict (INV-08); a newer statement of the same fact wins (C5).
7. **Applied adaptations:** recorded on their week and applied again, in order and with their own clocks (C4).
8. **Events:** one event per goal; a date change re-plans the remaining days it moves into or out of the event window and leaves no second race (C5, C7).
9. **Ceilings through schedule and time changes:** the caps rise or end only on a whole count after the time fit (D56); the return ceiling reads the largest whole count executed (R20).
10. **Observation and target:** an observation sets a target only when its exposure establishes the load; a known-load observation is progression evidence for the gate (QE-R7A-12).
